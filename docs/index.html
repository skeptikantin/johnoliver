John Oliver’s speech rate over time

2025-01-08

The mystery

I recently stumbled across an old John Oliver episode from Season 1. The pace of the episode was so noticeably slower as if I had YouTube on half speed. My interest was piqued: has John Oliver become more fast-paced over time?

For that question, it’s a rather straightforward corpus linguistic task: we can measure speech rate in words per minute or syllables per second, and look at the average duration of pauses - metrics which will probably approximate John’s speech style space over time.

The data

From a list of main segments of Last Week Tonight and their YouTube links, I fetched the audio, auto-generated subtitles, and some meta data such as file duration and views (using yt-dlp). Gentle aligns the audio to the subtitles and determine the beginning and end of each word. This is rather coarse-grained, and the only forced-aligner I could find that works on files larger than a few seconds - this comes at the cost of accuracy, but given the amount of data for the question to be answered, this was a workable trade-off.

Two issues: First, John Oliver frequently features clips with speech from other people, which would have to be removed. To assess, I manually filtered other people’s text from the files for 20 episodes, and, if anything, the reported effects would be more pronounced, hence I decided using all data is good enough for the current purpose, and treat other people’s speech as noise - as lots of data tends to make such problems be much less influential.
